Automating Trades on UAE Stock Markets: Questions to Ask About API Access

Investors trading on the Dubai Financial Market and Abu Dhabi Securities Exchange increasingly ask whether they can automate their orders. The answer depends almost entirely on the broker. Before planning any software, collect the facts on API access, because they set the limits of what can be built.

Does your broker offer an API?

Ask your broker in writing whether they provide an order API, a FIX connection, or neither, and to which clients. Some offer APIs only to professional or institutional accounts. If there is no API, automation is limited to alerts that you act on by hand.

Market data

Order access and price data are often separate. Ask where real-time prices come from, whether there is an extra fee, and whether you may use the data in your own software.

Testing environment

A test or paper trading environment lets you check the software without risking money. Ask whether one exists and how close it is to the live system.

Limits and order types

Ask for the request limits, the order types the API accepts, and how it reports rejections. Price limits and tick sizes on the exchanges also apply to automated orders, so the software has to round prices correctly.

Trading hours

Both exchanges publish their session times, including pre-open and closing auctions, and their holiday calendars. Use the published schedules in your code instead of fixed times.

Signals from TradingView or Telegram

Many investors want orders to follow TradingView alerts or a Telegram group. That adds symbol mapping, because chart symbols and broker codes rarely match, and protection against duplicate alerts.

Reports

If the people who review trades read Arabic, plan Arabic reports and alerts from the start.
